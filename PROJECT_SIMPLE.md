# Contract Atlas, simply

## Who is it for?

A small cybersecurity company that wants to do government work and find companies to partner with.

## What problem does it solve?

Research is scattered across government contract records, government documents, company websites, and previous conversations. People repeat the research and lose useful corrections.

## What does the app do?

You ask a question such as:

> “We implement identity and access management. Who could complement us on DHS work, and what evidence supports the fit?”

Three AI workers help:

1. A **researcher** looks at agency needs and past contracts.
2. A **partner analyst** looks for companies with relevant capabilities.
3. A **reviewer** checks the conclusions and points out weak evidence.

You can open the sources behind their findings.

## Where does Cognee fit?

Cognee is their shared memory. It connects the source material, stores the workers' findings, and lets one worker retrieve what another found.

You can also accept a lesson from the review. For example:

> “A company advertising a capability does not prove it delivered that work for DHS.”

Cognee's improvement step attempts to save that lesson for future research. We then start a fresh AI session and check whether it actually retrieves the saved lesson.

## What will judges see?

One app: ask a research question → watch the workers → inspect evidence → accept a lesson → see what a fresh worker remembered.

That directly demonstrates the track's two priorities: **shared agent memory** and **self-improvement**.

## What are we building in four hours?

A focused working demo with real past contract records, an official CISA page, two company capability pages, source links, and the review-and-memory loop. It helps with research and potential partners. It does not submit bids or establish that an opportunity is currently open.

The memory check is honest: if no lesson is saved or retrieved, the app says so.
